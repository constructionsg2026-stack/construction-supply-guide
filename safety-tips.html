01 Electrical

  • Assume every circuit is live until you test it.
  • Verify zero energy with a meter you know works.
  • Damaged cords and homemade splices are not temporary. They are incidents waiting for a date.

03 Fall protection

  • If your feet leave the ground and the drop can hurt you, plan the protection first.
  • Right harness, right anchor, right lanyard.
  • A harness in the truck is not fall protection.

06 Ladders

  • Right ladder, right height, right angle. Three points of contact.
  • Don’t stand on the top cap.
  • Inspect rails, feet, and spreaders before you climb. If it feels sketchy, it is.

08 Overhead power lines

  • Look up before you boom, raise, or carry long material.
  • Overhead lines don’t need to be touched to kill you.
  • If you don’t know the voltage, assume the worst and stay back.

15 Trenching

  • A trench can collapse faster than you can react.
  • Don’t enter an unprotected trench. Keep spoil back from the edge.
  • If the walls look pretty good, that isn’t engineering.

17 Silica dust

  • Cutting and grinding concrete makes silica dust that scars lungs.
  • Use wet methods or vacuums with the right filtration.
  • Never dry sweep it. Dust you can’t see can still hurt you.

26 Heat

  • Heat sneaks up. Drink water before you are thirsty. Rest in the shade.
  • Watch each other for anyone who just doesn’t look right.
  • No breaks on a roof in July isn’t a plan.

29 Hot work

  • Sparks travel farther than people think.
  • Clear combustibles, hang blankets, know where the extinguisher is.
  • Assign a fire watch that actually watches. Fires love to start after everyone has left.

37 Rigging

  • If you didn’t inspect the sling, don’t hook the load.
  • Know the weight. Know the center of gravity. Stay out from under it.
  • One qualified person runs the lift.

38 Forklifts

  • Blind spots are bigger than you think. Spotters help, but eye contact is the rule.
  • No riders. Seatbelts on.
  • Pedestrians: don’t assume the operator saw you.

40 Rebar

  • Uncapped rebar is a spear. Cap it, cover it, or bend it per the plan.
  • If you walked past open rebar and did nothing, you accepted the hazard for the next person.

46 Stop-work

  • You have the right and the duty to stop unsafe work.
  • You don’t need permission to keep someone from getting hurt.
  • A short delay is cheaper than an ambulance.
